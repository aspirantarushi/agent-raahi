# Free legal aid: who qualifies and how to apply

Module: all · Checked: 2026-11-02 · Lawyer checked: no (every entry below)

Note: the NALSA (Free and Competent Legal Services) Regulations, 2010 were amended in 2012, 2018 and 2019. Entries below follow the current, amended text.

### Who qualifies for free legal aid
A person who has to file or defend a case gets free legal services if they belong to any one of these groups:
- a member of a Scheduled Caste or Scheduled Tribe
- a victim of human trafficking or begar (forced labour)
- a woman or a child (anyone under 18)
- a person with a disability
- a person in "undeserved want", such as a victim of a mass disaster, ethnic violence, caste atrocity, flood, drought, earthquake or industrial disaster
- an industrial workman
- a person in custody, including in a protective home, juvenile home, or psychiatric hospital or nursing home
- a person whose annual income is below the limit set for their state (see income_limits.csv), or below ₹5 lakh for cases in the Supreme Court

Women, children and people in custody qualify whatever their income.
Source: Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987, Section 12
Link: https://sclsc.gov.in/legal-services/Entitlement

### The person must also have a case worth pursuing
Belonging to a group in Section 12 is not enough on its own. The legal services authority must also be satisfied that the person has a prima facie case (a case that looks genuine on first reading). An accused person, a convict who wants to appeal, and a defendant in a civil case are treated as already having a prima facie case to defend or appeal.
Source: Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987, Section 13(1); NALSA (Free and Competent Legal Services) Regulations, 2010, Regulation 7 (as substituted in 2018)
Link: https://nalsa.gov.in/regulations/

### A self-declaration is usually enough proof
To show they belong to a Section 12 group, a self-certificate by the applicant, with self-attested copies of any supporting documents, is ordinarily enough. For income, the Act says the person's own affidavit (sworn statement) can be accepted unless the authority has a reason to disbelieve it. No income certificate is required by law.
Source: NALSA (Free and Competent Legal Services) Regulations, 2010, Regulation 5 (as amended in 2018); Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987, Section 13(2)
Link: https://nalsa.gov.in/regulations/

### Some states add more groups
States can widen eligibility. For example, Bihar also gives free legal aid regardless of income to transgender persons, senior citizens, people with HIV or any type of cancer, unorganised sector workers and acid attack victims. Bihar's income limit is ₹1,50,000 a year, and the applicant must be a resident of Bihar. Check the State Legal Services Authority website for other states.
Source: Bihar State Legal Services Authority, Eligibility page (BSLSA Regulations, 1998, Regulation 19)
Link: https://patnahighcourt.gov.in/BSLSA/Eligibility.aspx

### Where to apply
Apply at the legal services institution for the court where the case is or will be:
- District Legal Services Authority (DLSA) front office, usually inside the district court complex
- Taluk / Sub-divisional Legal Services Committee for courts below district level
- High Court Legal Services Committee for High Court cases
- Supreme Court Legal Services Committee for Supreme Court cases
You can also call NALSA's free helpline 15100 or apply online on NALSA's portal, and receive a diary number to track the application. Every legal services institution must have a front office, staffed during office hours by a retainer lawyer (on rotation) and paralegal volunteers, which works as a one-stop centre for people seeking legal aid.
Source: NALSA (Free and Competent Legal Services) Regulations, 2010, Regulations 3–4 (as amended); NALSA "How to apply" page

### How the application is made
The application is preferably made in Form I, in the local language or English. A note of the problem can be attached on a separate sheet. An application in another format is still accepted if it explains the facts. People who cannot read or write can get help to fill it in. Oral requests are treated like written ones, and email and online requests can be accepted once identity is checked. Applications sent by paralegal volunteers, legal aid clinics and voluntary organisations must also be considered.
Source: NALSA (Free and Competent Legal Services) Regulations, 2010, Regulation 3
Link: https://nalsa.gov.in/regulations/

### What to carry
- Any identity proof
- The self-certificate (or income affidavit) showing eligibility, with self-attested copies of any supporting documents
- All case papers the person has: FIR copy, notices, court orders, chargesheet, case number (CNR number from eCourts)
Source: NALSA (Free and Competent Legal Services) Regulations, 2010, Regulation 5 (as amended in 2018)
Link: https://nalsa.gov.in/regulations/

### How long a decision takes
The Member-Secretary or Secretary of the legal services institution (or an officer they choose) checks the application. A decision must be taken immediately, and within seven days of receiving the application at most. (Before the 2018 amendment the limit was eight weeks.)
Source: NALSA (Free and Competent Legal Services) Regulations, 2010, Regulation 7 (as substituted in 2018); NALSA "Legal Aid" page
Link: https://nalsa.gov.in/legal-aid/

### If the application is refused
A person who is refused can appeal to the Executive Chairman or Chairman of the legal services authority; that decision is final. If the person is not eligible, the institution should point them to other organisations or schemes that give free legal help, from a list it keeps.
Source: NALSA (Free and Competent Legal Services) Regulations, 2010, Regulation 7 (as substituted in 2018)
Link: https://nalsa.gov.in/regulations/

### The legal aid lawyer cannot charge any fee
A panel lawyer assigned through legal aid must not take any fee or anything of value from the person. When a lawyer is assigned, the person's own choice of panel lawyer may be considered.
Source: NALSA (Free and Competent Legal Services) Regulations, 2010, Regulations 7 and 8 (as amended)
Link: https://nalsa.gov.in/regulations/

### If the legal aid lawyer is not doing the work
Each legal services institution has a Monitoring and Mentoring Committee that tracks legal aid cases and the panel lawyer's work, and meets at least once a fortnight. If a panel lawyer performs badly, the institution can take the case back and remove the lawyer from the panel. The person can write to the Member-Secretary (state authority) or Secretary (district authority) asking for another lawyer.
Source: NALSA (Free and Competent Legal Services) Regulations, 2010, Regulations 8 and 10–12 (as amended in 2018 and 2019)
Link: https://nalsa.gov.in/regulations/

### False information ends legal aid
If legal aid was obtained using false or fraudulent information, it stops at once and the costs can be recovered from the person.
Source: NALSA (Free and Competent Legal Services) Regulations, 2010, Regulation 6
Link: https://nalsa.gov.in/regulations/

### Free advice before any case is filed: Tele-Law
For advice before any case is filed, people can use Tele-Law: free consultation with a panel lawyer through the Tele-Law app, the toll-free number 14454, or video calls at Common Service Centres. Tele-Law also runs on WhatsApp through the Nyaya Setu chatbot (7217711814).
Source: Department of Justice, DISHA scheme: Tele-Law and the toll-free helpline 14454 (Press Information Bureau release); the Nyaya Setu WhatsApp number is to be confirmed on an official page
