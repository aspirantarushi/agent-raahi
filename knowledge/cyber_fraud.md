# Cyber and financial fraud: first response

### Call 1930 at once: report immediately
If money has been taken through online fraud (UPI, card, net banking, wallet), call the national cyber-fraud helpline 1930 immediately, or report it on cybercrime.gov.in. Report immediately: the reporting system exists so that the money can be stopped before the fraudsters siphon it off.
Source: Ministry of Home Affairs, Press Information Bureau release on the Citizen Financial Cyber Fraud Reporting and Management System
Link: https://pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2110809

### What happens after a 1930 report
The complaint is sent to the bank or payment company involved. If the money is still in the receiving account, the bank puts a hold on it so it can't be withdrawn. If it has already moved, the next bank in the chain is asked to hold it, and so on. Banks then tell the police how much was blocked.
Source: Ministry of Home Affairs, Press Information Bureau release on the Citizen Financial Cyber Fraud Reporting and Management System
Link: https://pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2110809

### Getting held money back usually needs a court order
Money that has been frozen is usually returned only after a court allows it. The police investigating the case, or a lawyer (including a free legal aid lawyer), can help apply to the magistrate's court for its release.
Source: Moneylife (Bengaluru case: money returned after court permission)

### Tell the bank in writing within 3 working days (transactions up to 31 December 2026)
These rules apply to transactions made up to 31 December 2026. New rules apply from 1 January 2027 (see "New RBI rules for transactions from 1 January 2027" below). If money left the account without the person's permission, and the fault lies neither with the person nor the bank (for example a hacked card or a data breach elsewhere), the person loses nothing if they report it to the bank within 3 working days of the bank's SMS or email about the transaction. Reported within 4 to 7 working days, their loss for each transaction is capped at that transaction's amount or a fixed limit, whichever is lower: ₹5,000 for basic savings (BSBD) accounts, ₹10,000 for most other savings accounts, ₹25,000 for larger current accounts and high-limit credit cards. After 7 working days, the bank's own policy decides.
If the bank itself was at fault, the person bears no loss at all, whenever they report.
Source: Reserve Bank of India (Commercial Banks – Responsible Business Conduct) Directions, 2025, paragraphs 67–69 (these carry forward the RBI circular of 6 July 2017, which was folded into the new directions in November 2025)
Link: https://rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_ViewMasDirections.aspx?id=13140

### If the person shared their OTP or PIN
If the loss happened because the person was careless, for example by sharing an OTP, PIN or password, they bear the loss until they report it to the bank. Any loss after reporting falls on the bank. This is why reporting immediately still matters.
Source: RBI Responsible Business Conduct Directions, 2025, Section D (customer negligence provision, carried forward from the 2017 circular); the same rule continues from 1 January 2027
Link: https://rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_ViewMasDirections.aspx?id=13140

### The bank must re-credit the money within 10 working days (transactions up to 31 December 2026)
Once the person reports an unauthorised transaction, the bank must put the disputed amount back in their account within 10 working days (a "shadow reversal"), without waiting for any insurance claim, while it investigates. The complaint must be resolved within 90 days. The bank has to prove the customer was at fault, not the other way round.
Source: RBI Responsible Business Conduct Directions, 2025, paragraphs 72–73 (carried forward from the 2017 circular)
Link: https://rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_ViewMasDirections.aspx?id=13140

### New RBI rules for transactions from 1 January 2027
On 24 June 2026 the RBI issued new rules for fraudulent electronic banking transactions made on or after 1 January 2027:
- Bank at fault: the customer loses nothing, whenever they report.
- Neither the customer nor the bank at fault (third-party breach): the customer loses nothing only if they report within 5 calendar days of the date the fraud happened. After that, the bank's own policy decides. The 4–7 working day caps no longer apply.
- Customer careless (for example shared an OTP): the customer bears the loss until they report it; losses after reporting fall on the bank. A transaction the customer made because a fraudster coerced or pressured them also counts as fraud.
- One-time compensation: an individual (including a sole proprietor) whose gross loss was up to ₹50,000 because of their own carelessness can get 85% of the net loss or ₹25,000, whichever is less, once in their lifetime, if they report to 1930 or cybercrime.gov.in and to the bank within 5 calendar days of the fraud. Companies are not covered. The bank must pay within 5 calendar days of a complete claim. This covers frauds in 2027 (one year from 1 January 2027).
- For credit card fraud, the bank must give a temporary credit (a "shadow reversal") within 5 calendar days of the report; any reversal is dated back to the transaction date, with no lost interest or extra charges.
- The bank must still prove the customer was at fault, and must resolve the complaint within 45 calendar days (60 for cross-border fraud).
Source: Reserve Bank of India (Commercial Banks – Responsible Business Conduct) Third Amendment Directions, 2026 (RBI/2026-27/167, 24 June 2026), paragraphs 76K–76U; similar directions issued for small finance, payments, regional rural and co-operative banks
Link: https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Id=13543

### If the bank doesn't respond
The person must complain to the bank first and keep proof of that complaint. If the bank doesn't reply within 30 days, or the person is unhappy with its reply, they can complain free of charge to the RBI Ombudsman under the Reserve Bank – Integrated Ombudsman Scheme, 2026 (in force since 1 July 2026). The complaint must reach the Ombudsman within 90 days of the end of the bank's 30-day reply period, or of the bank's last reply, whichever is later. Complain online at cms.rbi.org.in, by email to crpc@rbi.org.in, or by post to the Centralised Receipt and Processing Centre, RBI, Sector 17, Chandigarh 160017. The toll-free number 14448 gives guidance and status updates but cannot take complaints. The Ombudsman can award up to ₹30 lakh for consequential loss and up to ₹3 lakh for harassment and expenses. (Under the old 2021 scheme the time limit was one year; that scheme still covers complaints filed before 1 July 2026.)
Source: RBI, Frequently Asked Questions on the Reserve Bank – Integrated Ombudsman Scheme, 2026
Link: https://www.rbi.org.in/commonman/Upload/English/FAQs/PDFs/RBIOS01072026.pdf

### Stop further loss straight away
Ask the bank to block the debit or credit card, UPI and net banking on the affected account, and change passwords and UPI PINs from a safe phone. Do not answer further calls from the fraudster.
Source: General guidance; RBI Responsible Business Conduct Directions, 2025, Section D (banks must provide 24x7 channels to report and block)
Link: https://rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_ViewMasDirections.aspx?id=13140

### Evidence to keep
Keep, and don't delete:
- the date and time of each transaction, amount, and transaction ID or UTR number
- the bank SMS and email alerts
- the fraudster's phone numbers, UPI IDs, account numbers, website links or app names
- screenshots of chats, call logs and payment screens
- the 1930 or cybercrime.gov.in complaint (acknowledgement) number
Source: cybercrime.gov.in reporting form fields
Link: https://cybercrime.gov.in/

### "Digital arrest" calls are always fraud
No police, CBI, ED, customs officer or judge arrests anyone over a video call or asks for money to avoid arrest. Callers who claim the person or a relative is involved in drugs or money laundering, keep them on camera, and demand payment as "bail" are fraudsters. Hang up, don't pay, and report to 1930 or cybercrime.gov.in.
Source: Indian Cyber Crime Coordination Centre advisory (Deccan Herald report)

### Which laws apply
Online fraud is usually charged as cheating (BNS Section 318; IPC 420 for offences before 1 July 2024) together with the Information Technology Act, 2000: Section 66C (identity theft, such as misusing someone's password) and Section 66D (cheating by impersonation using a computer or phone).
Source: Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023, Section 318; Information Technology Act, 2000, Sections 66C and 66D

### Free legal help for fraud victims
Women, senior citizens in some states (such as Bihar), and people under the income limit can get a free legal aid lawyer to help get frozen money released or to follow up the police case. Apply at the District Legal Services Authority or call 15100. See legal_aid_eligibility.md.
Source: Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987, Section 12; BSLSA eligibility page
