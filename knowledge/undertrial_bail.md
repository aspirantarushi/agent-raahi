# Arrest, bail and undertrials

Module: undertrial and bail · Checked: 2026-11-02 · Lawyer checked: no (every entry below)

Old and new law: offences committed before 1 July 2024 are charged under the Indian Penal Code (IPC). From 1 July 2024 the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS) defines offences and the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS) sets procedure, replacing the Code of Criminal Procedure (CrPC). Always ask the date of the offence first, and check with a lawyer which procedure applies to an older case.

---

### Rights on arrest
A person being arrested has these rights:
- For offences punishable with up to 7 years, the police should usually issue a notice to appear instead of arresting straight away (BNSS 35(3)).
- The arresting officer must be identifiable and prepare an arrest memo (BNSS 36).
- To be told the grounds of arrest (BNSS 47(1)), and, for a bailable offence, that they are entitled to bail (BNSS 47(2)).
- To have a relative, friend or chosen person told about the arrest and where they are held (BNSS 48).
- To meet an advocate of their choice during interrogation, though not for the whole of it (BNSS 38).
- To ask for a medical examination by a registered doctor to record injuries (BNSS 53).
- To be produced before a magistrate within 24 hours, not counting travel time (BNSS 58).
- A woman should not be arrested between sunset and sunrise, except in exceptional cases with a woman officer's written report and a magistrate's prior permission (BNSS 43(5)).
- Not to be forced to be a witness against themselves (Constitution, Article 20(3)), and to consult a lawyer (Article 22(1)).
Source: BNSS, 2023, Sections 35(3), 36, 38, 43(5), 47(1), 47(2), 48, 53 and 58; Constitution of India, Articles 20(3) and 22(1)

### Free legal aid starts at the first court appearance
An accused person who cannot afford a lawyer is entitled to free legal aid from the moment they are first produced before a magistrate, and the magistrate must tell them about this right. They do not need to have asked for it. In jails, prison legal aid clinics and the District Legal Services Authority (DLSA) provide this help.
Source: Khatri (II) v. State of Bihar (1981), Supreme Court (Digital SCR); Suk Das v. Union Territory of Arunachal Pradesh (1986) (secondary: official copy not yet found); Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987, Section 12(g)

### Bailable offences: bail is a right
For a bailable offence, bail is a right and the police or court must grant it. A poor person may be released on a bond without a surety. If the person cannot give bail within one week of arrest, the court can treat this as enough reason to presume they are indigent (too poor) and release them on a personal bond.
Source: BNSS, 2023, Section 478

### Non-bailable offences: bail is the court's decision
For a non-bailable offence, the court decides whether to grant bail. It is usually refused where there are reasonable grounds to believe the person committed an offence punishable with death or life imprisonment, or where certain past convictions exist. Even then, a child, a woman, or a sick or infirm person may be released. The public prosecutor must be heard for offences punishable with death, life imprisonment or 7 years or more.
Source: BNSS, 2023, Section 480

### Bail before arrest (anticipatory bail)
A person who fears arrest for a non-bailable offence can ask the High Court or Court of Session for anticipatory bail. It is not available for rape of a girl under 16 (BNS Section 65) or gang rape of a girl under 18 (BNS Section 70(2)).
Source: BNSS, 2023, Section 482

### Default bail if police don't finish investigating in time
If police don't file the chargesheet in time, the accused gets a right to bail on furnishing it ("default bail"). The limit is 90 days for offences punishable with death, life imprisonment or 10 years or more, and 60 days for other offences. The accused must apply for it before the chargesheet is filed.
Source: BNSS, 2023, Section 187(3)

### Section 479: release after a share of the maximum sentence
An undertrial (a person in jail whose trial isn't finished) must be released:
- on bail, once they have been in custody for half of the maximum prison term for the offence, or
- on a personal bond (no surety needed), once they have been in custody for one-third of the maximum term, if they have never been convicted of any offence before (a "first-time offender").
Custody during investigation, inquiry and trial all counts. Delay caused by the accused is not counted. No undertrial can be held beyond the full maximum term.
Source: BNSS, 2023, Section 479

### When Section 479 does not apply
- The offence can be punished with death or life imprisonment.
- Investigation, inquiry or trial is pending against the person for more than one offence, or in more than one case (Section 479(2)). They can still apply for ordinary bail.
After hearing the public prosecutor and recording reasons, the court can also keep the person in custody beyond half the term, or attach conditions instead of a bare bond.
Source: BNSS, 2023, Section 479

### Several sections in one case may also block Section 479
Section 479(2) bars release where the person faces more than one offence, not only more than one case. Read literally, one FIR charging two or more sections (for example robbery and causing hurt) may be enough to block release; commentators call this an unsettled point, along with whether the life-imprisonment exclusion and this bar apply to cases from before 1 July 2024. Courts are applying the bar strictly where there are several cases: the Karnataka High Court (K. Ramakrishna v. Assistant Director, 2024) read Sections 479(1) and 479(2) together and refused relief to an accused facing multiple cases. As of October 2026 the Supreme Court had still not settled the single-case question. Whenever more than one section is charged, the agent must not give a Section 479 date as settled: it should flag the case for a lawyer.
Source: BNSS, 2023, Section 479(2); Karnataka High Court ruling of 23 November 2024 (secondary: official copy not yet found)

### Who applies under Section 479
The jail superintendent has a legal duty to apply in writing to the court once the one-third or one-half period is complete. The prisoner or their lawyer can also apply. Families should work through the legal aid lawyer, the jail legal aid clinic or the District Legal Services Authority (DLSA).
Source: BNSS, 2023, Section 479(3)

### Section 479 applies to older cases too
In August 2024 the Supreme Court held that Section 479 applies to undertrials whose cases began before the BNSS came into force on 1 July 2024, and told jail superintendents to identify eligible prisoners. In a later order (19 November 2024) it said special efforts should be made to identify eligible women prisoners, and that jail superintendents should personally attend to them.
Source: In Re: Inhuman Conditions in 1382 Prisons, W.P. (C) 406/2013 (Supreme Court orders of 23 August 2024 and 19 November 2024; official copy not yet found); Ministry of Home Affairs advisory of 16 October 2024

### How to work out the Section 479 dates
Use the calculator, never mental arithmetic. The steps it follows:
1. Find the offence's maximum prison term in sentence_table.csv.
2. If the table says death or life imprisonment is possible, Section 479 does not apply.
3. Half the maximum term, counted from the date custody began, gives the bail date.
4. For a first-time offender, one-third of the maximum term gives the bond date.
5. If more than one case is pending, Section 479 release is not available. If more than one section is charged in the same case, show the dates only as "to be checked by a lawyer", because Section 479(2) may bar release.
Example: theft in a dwelling house (BNS 305, maximum 7 years = 84 months). A first-time offender in custody since 1 March 2025 reaches one-third (28 months) on 1 July 2027.
Source: BNSS, 2023, Section 479; BNS, 2023, Section 305

### Old offences use the old maximum
For an offence committed before 1 July 2024, the maximum prison term is the one in the Indian Penal Code (IPC), not the BNS. The Constitution says no one can be given a penalty greater than the law allowed when the offence was committed. For most offences the two maximums are the same, but some differ: for example, cheating under IPC 417 was up to 1 year, while BNS 318(2) allows up to 3 years. The ipc_max_prison_months column of sentence_table.csv gives the IPC maximum.
Source: Constitution of India, Article 20(1); Indian Penal Code, 1860 (section texts)

### When the maximum depends on the facts
Some offences have different maximums depending on what happened. For example, attempt to murder is up to 10 years, but life imprisonment if hurt was caused; theft is up to 3 years for a first conviction but up to 5 years for a later one. The calculator must ask the questions that decide which row applies. If unsure, use the higher maximum and tell the volunteer to confirm with a lawyer.
Source: BNS, 2023, Sections 109 and 303(2)

### Bail granted but still in jail
Many undertrials stay in jail after bail is granted because they cannot arrange sureties or bail money. In such cases, the lawyer can ask the court to reduce the bail amount or accept a personal bond, citing Section 478 (indigent persons) and the Supreme Court's bail guidelines in Satender Kumar Antil v. CBI (2022). The DLSA can help. In January 2023, NALSA told the Supreme Court that about 5,000 undertrials were in this situation and only 1,417 had been released.
Source: Deccan Herald report on NALSA's submission to the Supreme Court; BNSS Section 478

### Who to approach for an undertrial
- The jail's legal aid clinic (run by the DLSA inside the prison)
- The legal aid lawyer assigned to the case
- The DLSA at the district court, or NALSA's helpline 15100
- The Undertrial Review Committee, chaired by the District Judge, which reviews undertrial cases for release

### Special laws are not covered yet
Offences under special laws, such as the Narcotic Drugs and Psychotropic Substances (NDPS) Act, the Arms Act, or state prohibition and excise laws (for example the Bihar Prohibition and Excise Act, 2016), have their own punishments and bail rules. They are not in sentence_table.csv yet. Send these cases to a lawyer.
