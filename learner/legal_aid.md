## Explainer (read aloud, about 2 minutes)
India's Constitution (Article 39A) promises equal justice and free legal aid. The Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987 makes this real. About 80% of Indians qualify, yet only about 15.5 lakh people got free legal services in 2023–24, and about 16.6 lakh in 2025–26 up to January 2026 (Law Ministry replies in the Lok Sabha).

Who qualifies? Women and children, Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes, people with disabilities, victims of trafficking or disasters, industrial workers, and anyone in custody, whatever their income. Others qualify if their annual income is below their state's limit, which is ₹1–3 lakh in most states and ₹5 lakh for Supreme Court cases. A simple self-declaration is usually enough proof. The case must also look genuine, and an accused person is treated as having a case to defend.

Where to go? The District Legal Services Authority (DLSA) front office in the district court, the NALSA helpline 15100, or NALSA's online application. The application is preferably on Form I, but any request that explains the facts is accepted, including oral requests, and people who can't read or write can get help filling it in. A decision must be taken immediately, within seven days at most. If refused, the person can appeal to the authority's chairman.

Free legal aid covers a lawyer, drafting, court fees and copies of documents, and the legal aid lawyer cannot charge any fee. If the lawyer isn't working on the case, the person can ask the DLSA for a different lawyer.

## Quiz (10 questions)

1. Which law sets out who gets free legal aid?
   a) Advocates Act, 1961  b) Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987  c) Right to Information Act, 2005  d) Consumer Protection Act, 2019
   Answer: b. Section 12.

2. A woman earning ₹8 lakh a year needs a lawyer. Does she qualify?
   a) No  b) Yes, women qualify whatever their income  c) Only in Delhi  d) Only for criminal cases
   Answer: b. Section 12(c).

3. What is usually enough to prove income?
   a) An income tax return  b) A salary slip  c) The person's own affidavit or self-declaration  d) A bank statement for 3 years
   Answer: c. Section 13(2) of the Act and Regulation 5 (as amended in 2018), unless the authority has reason to disbelieve it.

4. What else must the authority be satisfied about?
   a) That the person has a prima facie case  b) That the person has a degree  c) That the person owns property  d) Nothing else
   Answer: a. Section 13(1).

5. What is Bihar's income limit for free legal aid?
   a) ₹1,00,000  b) ₹1,50,000  c) ₹3,00,000  d) ₹5,00,000
   Answer: b. BSLSA Regulations, 1998, Regulation 19.

6. How quickly must a legal aid application be decided?
   a) Immediately, within 7 days at most  b) 8 weeks  c) 6 months  d) No limit
   Answer: a. NALSA Regulations 2010, Regulation 7 as substituted in 2018 (it used to be eight weeks).

7. The application is refused. What can the person do?
   a) Nothing  b) Appeal to the Executive Chairman or Chairman of the authority  c) File a writ in the Supreme Court only  d) Reapply after a year
   Answer: b. Regulation 7.

8. What is the NALSA legal aid helpline number?
   a) 1930  b) 112  c) 15100  d) 1098
   Answer: c.

9. Someone needs advice but has no court case yet. Which free service fits best?
   a) Tele-Law (14454)  b) RBI Ombudsman  c) 1098  d) Lok Sabha
   Answer: a.

10. The legal aid lawyer never turns up. What can the person do?
    a) Nothing  b) Ask the DLSA to assign another lawyer  c) Pay a private lawyer only  d) Withdraw the case
    Answer: b. The authority can withdraw the case from a lawyer who performs badly (Regulation 8).

## Practice case 1 (basics)
Case file for the agent to play:
- Speaker: a domestic worker in Delhi, a man aged 40.
- Facts (reveal only when asked): his employer has not paid him for 3 months; he earns about ₹1.2 lakh a year; he belongs to a Scheduled Caste; no case filed yet.
Model answer:
- He qualifies twice over: Scheduled Caste (Section 12(a)), and income below Delhi's ₹3 lakh limit.
- No case yet: start with free advice from Tele-Law (14454) or the DLSA front office; the DLSA can assign a lawyer if a case is needed.
- Draft the DLSA application (templates/dlsa_application.md) with a short statement of the unpaid wages.
- Must not: tell him he will definitely win.

## Practice case 2 (advanced)
Case file for the agent to play:
- Speaker: an NGO caseworker in Gujarat.
- Facts: a man aged 35, income ₹1.8 lakh a year, not in any Section 12 group, wants a lawyer to defend a civil suit over a small shop rent dispute.
Model answer:
- Not in a Section 12 group; Gujarat's limit in the NALSA table is ₹1 lakh, so on income alone he may not qualify. Confirm the current limit with the Gujarat SLSA, since state limits change.
- If refused: he can appeal to the authority's chairman, and the institution should point him to other free legal help.
- Alternatives: Tele-Law for advice; Lok Adalat or mediation for the rent dispute.
- Must not: discourage him from applying; the authority decides.
