## You will be able to
- Tell whether a person qualifies for free legal aid, and say why.
- Help them apply to the right legal services office, and explain what happens next.

## Key idea
1. Free legal aid is for a person who has to file or defend a case and belongs to a group in Section 12: a member of a Scheduled Caste or Scheduled Tribe, a victim of trafficking or begar, a woman or a child, a person with a disability, a person in undeserved want (such as a disaster victim), an industrial workman, a person in custody, or a person whose income is below their state's limit.
   Source: Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987, Section 12 [[Who qualifies for free legal aid]]
2. Women, children, people in custody, SC/ST members, people with disabilities, industrial workers, and victims of trafficking or disasters qualify whatever their income. Others qualify if their income is below their state's limit (or below ₹5 lakh for cases in the Supreme Court).
   Source: Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987, Section 12 [[Who qualifies for free legal aid]]
3. The authority must also be satisfied that the person has a prima facie case. An accused person, a convict who wants to appeal, and a defendant in a civil case are treated as already having one.
   Source: Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987, Section 13(1) [[The person must also have a case worth pursuing]]
4. A self-certificate is ordinarily enough proof of belonging to a group, and the person's own affidavit of income can be accepted. No income certificate is required by law.
   Source: Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987, Section 13(2); NALSA Regulations, 2010, Regulation 5 [[A self-declaration is usually enough proof]]
5. A decision must be taken immediately, and within seven days of receiving the application at most. A person who is refused can appeal to the Executive Chairman or Chairman of the legal services authority.
   Source: NALSA (Free and Competent Legal Services) Regulations, 2010, Regulation 7 [[How long a decision takes]] [[If the application is refused]]

## Worked example
A woman in Bihar earns ₹2 lakh a year. She has been sued in a civil case and needs a lawyer to defend it.
1. Does she have a case worth pursuing? Yes: a defendant in a civil case is treated as already having a prima facie case to defend.
   Source: Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987, Section 13(1) [[The person must also have a case worth pursuing]]
2. Is she in a Section 12 group? Yes: she is a woman, and women qualify whatever their income. So it does not matter that ₹2 lakh is above Bihar's ₹1,50,000 limit.
   Source: Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987, Section 12 [[Who qualifies for free legal aid]] [[Some states add more groups]]
3. What proof does she need? A self-certificate, with self-attested copies of any supporting documents. No income certificate.
   Source: NALSA Regulations, 2010, Regulation 5 [[A self-declaration is usually enough proof]]
4. Where does she apply? The DLSA front office, usually inside the district court complex, or NALSA's free helpline 15100. She carries any identity proof, the self-certificate and all her case papers.
   Source: NALSA Regulations, 2010, Regulations 3–5 [[Where to apply]] [[What to carry]]
5. What happens next? A decision must be taken immediately, within seven days at most. If she is refused, she can appeal to the authority's Executive Chairman or Chairman.
   Source: NALSA Regulations, 2010, Regulation 7 [[How long a decision takes]] [[If the application is refused]]

## Practice case 1: a worker with unpaid wages (basics)
Core facts:
- A domestic worker in Delhi, a man aged 40.
- His employer has not paid him for 3 months. No case has been filed yet.
- He earns about ₹1.2 lakh a year.
- He belongs to a Scheduled Caste.
Model answer:
- He qualifies: he is a member of a Scheduled Caste (Section 12). His income is also below Delhi's ₹3,00,000 limit in our table; Delhi has separate limits for some groups, so confirm with the Delhi State Legal Services Authority. [[Who qualifies for free legal aid]]
- No case has been filed yet: for advice first, Tele-Law (14454) gives free consultation with a panel lawyer. The DLSA front office is the one-stop centre if a case is needed. [[Free advice before any case is filed: Tele-Law]] [[Where to apply]]
- Prepare the DLSA application with a short note of the unpaid wages. [[How the application is made]]
- Must not: tell him he will win.

## Practice case 2: income above the limit (advanced)
Core facts:
- A man aged 35 in Gujarat, not in any Section 12 group.
- He earns ₹1.8 lakh a year.
- He needs a lawyer to defend a civil suit over a small shop's rent.
Model answer:
- He is a defendant, so he is treated as having a case to defend. [[The person must also have a case worth pursuing]]
- He is not in a Section 12 group, and Gujarat's limit in our table is ₹1,00,000, so on income alone he may not qualify. State limits change: confirm with the Gujarat State Legal Services Authority. [[Who qualifies for free legal aid]]
- He can still apply; the authority decides. If refused, he can appeal to the authority's Executive Chairman or Chairman, and the office should point him to other free legal help from the list it keeps. [[If the application is refused]]
- For advice meanwhile: Tele-Law (14454). [[Free advice before any case is filed: Tele-Law]]
- Must not: discourage him from applying.

## Check (5 questions)
1. A woman earning ₹8 lakh a year needs a lawyer to defend a case. Does she qualify for free legal aid?
   a) No, her income is too high  b) Yes, women qualify whatever their income  c) Only in Delhi  d) Only for criminal cases
   Answer: b
   Why: Women, children, people in custody, SC/ST members, people with disabilities, industrial workers, and victims of trafficking or disasters qualify whatever their income.
   Source: Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987, Section 12 [[Who qualifies for free legal aid]]

2. What is ordinarily enough to show the person's income?
   a) An income tax return  b) An income certificate from the tehsil  c) The person's own affidavit  d) Three years of bank statements
   Answer: c
   Why: The person's own affidavit can be accepted unless the authority has a reason to disbelieve it. No income certificate is required by law.
   Source: Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987, Section 13(2) [[A self-declaration is usually enough proof]]

3. How quickly must a legal aid application be decided?
   a) Immediately, and within seven days at most  b) Within eight weeks  c) Within six months  d) There is no time limit
   Answer: a
   Why: The 2018 amendment set this limit; before it, the limit was eight weeks.
   Source: NALSA (Free and Competent Legal Services) Regulations, 2010, Regulation 7 [[How long a decision takes]]

4. A legal aid application is refused. What can the person do?
   a) Nothing  b) Appeal to the Executive Chairman or Chairman of the authority  c) Only file a case in the Supreme Court  d) Wait a year and reapply
   Answer: b
   Why: The person can appeal to the Executive Chairman or Chairman; that decision is final.
   Source: NALSA (Free and Competent Legal Services) Regulations, 2010, Regulation 7 [[If the application is refused]]

5. The legal aid lawyer asks the person for ₹2,000 "for expenses". What is right?
   a) Pay it: lawyers charge fees  b) The lawyer must not take any fee or anything of value  c) Pay half  d) Pay it only if the case is won
   Answer: b
   Why: A panel lawyer assigned through legal aid must not take any fee or anything of value from the person.
   Source: NALSA (Free and Competent Legal Services) Regulations, 2010, Regulations 7 and 8 [[The legal aid lawyer cannot charge any fee]]
