# Learner module: cyber and financial fraud

Lawyer checked: no. Facts here must match knowledge/cyber_fraud.md.

## Explainer (read aloud, about 2 minutes)
In 2025, Indians reported over 24 lakh cyber-fraud complaints and about ₹22,495 crore lost. Some of it can be saved, but only if people act fast.

The first step is always the same: call 1930, the national cyber-fraud helpline, or report on cybercrime.gov.in. The report goes to the banks involved, which can hold the money before the fraudster withdraws it. Every hour counts.

Next, tell the bank in writing. RBI rules protect customers: if money left the account without the customer's permission and it wasn't their fault or the bank's, they lose nothing if they report within 3 working days of the bank's alert. Between 4 and 7 working days, the loss for each transaction is capped. The bank must put the money back within 10 working days while it investigates, and it has to prove the customer was at fault. If the customer shared an OTP or PIN, they carry the loss until they report it, so reporting quickly still matters.

New RBI rules apply to transactions from 1 January 2027: the no-loss window becomes 5 calendar days from the transaction, banks must resolve complaints within 45 days, and people who lost up to ₹50,000 because they were tricked into sharing details can get one-time compensation of 85% of the loss, up to ₹25,000, if they report to 1930 and the bank within 5 days.

Finally, keep every piece of evidence, and remember: no police or government agency ever arrests anyone over a video call. "Digital arrest" calls are always fraud.

## Quiz (10 questions)

1. What is the very first thing to do after losing money to online fraud?
   a) Visit the bank branch next week  b) Call 1930 or report on cybercrime.gov.in  c) Post about it on social media  d) Wait for the fraudster to call back
   Answer: b. Fast reporting lets banks hold the money.

2. Why does speed matter after reporting to 1930?
   a) Fines go up later  b) Banks can hold the money before it is withdrawn  c) The case closes after a day  d) It doesn't matter
   Answer: b.

3. A hacked card is used without the customer's fault. They report it to the bank on day 2. What is their liability?
   a) Zero  b) ₹10,000  c) The full amount  d) Half
   Answer: a. Third-party breach reported within 3 working days: zero liability.

4. Same case, but reported on day 6. What is the most the customer can lose for each transaction (normal savings account)?
   a) Nothing  b) The lower of the transaction amount and ₹10,000  c) ₹25,000  d) The full amount
   Answer: b. RBI Responsible Business Conduct Directions, 2025, Table 1 (rules for transactions up to 31 December 2026).

5. Within how many working days must the bank credit the disputed amount back?
   a) 3  b) 7  c) 10  d) 90
   Answer: c. 10 working days, while it investigates.

6. The customer shared their OTP with a fake bank caller. Who bears the loss before they report it?
   a) The bank  b) The customer  c) The government  d) The fraudster's bank
   Answer: b. Losses after reporting fall on the bank.

7. A caller on video says he is a CBI officer and demands money to stop an arrest. What is it?
   a) A real arrest  b) A "digital arrest" fraud  c) A court summons  d) A tax notice
   Answer: b. No agency arrests people over a video call (I4C advisory).

8. The bank hasn't replied to the complaint in 30 days. Where can the customer complain free of charge?
   a) RBI Ombudsman at cms.rbi.org.in  b) Consumer helpline only  c) Nowhere  d) The Supreme Court directly
   Answer: a. Reserve Bank – Integrated Ombudsman Scheme, 2026 (file within 90 days of the bank's reply period ending).

9. Which of these should the victim keep?
   a) Transaction IDs/UTR numbers  b) Bank SMS alerts  c) Screenshots of chats  d) All of these
   Answer: d.

10. Money has been frozen in the fraudster's account. How is it usually returned?
    a) Automatically the next day  b) After a court allows it  c) By the fraudster  d) It can never be returned
    Answer: b. A free legal aid lawyer can help apply.

## Practice case 1 (basics)
Case file for the agent to play:
- Speaker: a 68-year-old retired teacher from Patna, anxious.
- Facts (reveal only when asked): two hours ago a man claiming to be from her bank said her account would be frozen; she shared an OTP; ₹48,000 left her savings account by UPI in two transactions (₹30,000 and ₹18,000); she got SMS alerts; she hasn't called anyone yet; she has the caller's number and screenshots of the SMS.
Model answer:
- Urgent: call 1930 now, before anything else.
- Ask the bank to block UPI and the card; send the written bank letter today (templates/bank_letter.md).
- Because she shared the OTP, she bears the loss up to the moment she reports; reporting now stops any further loss.
- Keep the evidence; prepare the complaint summary (templates/cyber_complaint.md).
- Free legal aid: in Bihar, senior citizens qualify whatever their income, and women qualify everywhere; a legal aid lawyer can help get frozen money released.
- If this happens on or after 1 January 2027: her loss is under ₹50,000, so if she reports to 1930 and the bank within 5 calendar days she can claim RBI's one-time compensation (85% of the net loss, up to ₹25,000).
- Must not: promise she will get the money back.

## Practice case 2 (advanced)
Case file for the agent to play:
- Speaker: a law-student volunteer helping a classmate.
- Facts: the classmate's debit card was used for three online purchases (₹6,000, ₹7,000, ₹9,000) she didn't make; she never shared any details; she got the SMS alerts 5 working days ago and is only now reporting; normal savings account.
Model answer:
- Third-party breach, reported in the 4–7 working-day window.
- Liability for each transaction is the lower of the transaction amount and ₹10,000, so at most ₹6,000 + ₹7,000 + ₹9,000 = ₹22,000; the exact outcome depends on the bank's finding. The bank must credit the disputed amount within 10 working days while it investigates.
- Steps: 1930 / cybercrime.gov.in; written bank letter today; block the card; keep evidence.
- If the bank doesn't respond in 30 days: RBI Ombudsman, within the next 90 days.
- Flag for the lawyer: confirm how the bank applies the per-transaction cap.
- Under the 2027 rules this would be different: 5 working days is more than 5 calendar days, so zero liability would be lost and the bank's own policy would decide.
