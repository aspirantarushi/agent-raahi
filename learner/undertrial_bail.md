## You will be able to
- Explain an undertrial's right to a free lawyer and to bail, in plain words.
- Work out with the calculator when Section 479 may apply, and pass the case to the lawyer without promising release.

## Key idea
1. An accused person who cannot afford a lawyer is entitled to free legal aid from the moment they are first produced before a magistrate, and the magistrate must tell them about this right. They do not need to have asked for it.
   Source: Khatri (II) v. State of Bihar (1981); Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987, Section 12(g) [[Free legal aid starts at the first court appearance]]
2. For a bailable offence, bail is a right. If the person cannot give bail within one week of arrest, the court can presume they are too poor and release them on a personal bond.
   Source: BNSS, 2023, Section 478 [[Bailable offences: bail is a right]]
3. Section 479: an undertrial must be released on bail after custody of half the maximum prison term for the offence, or on a personal bond after one-third if they have never been convicted of any offence before. Delay caused by the accused is not counted.
   Source: BNSS, 2023, Section 479 [[Section 479: release after a share of the maximum sentence]]
4. Section 479 does not apply if the offence can be punished with death or life imprisonment, or if an investigation, inquiry or trial is pending for more than one offence, or in more than one case.
   Source: BNSS, 2023, Section 479(2) [[When Section 479 does not apply]]
5. Release is not automatic: the jail superintendent has a legal duty to apply in writing to the court, the prisoner or their lawyer can also apply, and the court decides.
   Source: BNSS, 2023, Section 479(3) [[Who applies under Section 479]] [[When Section 479 does not apply]]

## Worked example
A man was arrested on 15 March 2024 for an offence in February 2024 and charged only under IPC 380 (theft in a dwelling house). He has never been convicted before and has no other case.
1. When was the offence? February 2024, before 1 July 2024, so the IPC maximum applies.
   Source: Constitution of India, Article 20(1) [[Old offences use the old maximum]]
2. What is the maximum? IPC 380 is BNS 305; the maximum in our sentence table is 84 months (7 years), and death or life imprisonment is not possible, so Section 479 can apply.
   Source: BNSS, 2023, Section 479; BNS, 2023, Section 305 [[How to work out the Section 479 dates]]
3. Do the bars apply? One case and one section, so no.
   Source: BNSS, 2023, Section 479(2) [[When Section 479 does not apply]]
4. The dates, with the calculator: one-third of 84 months is 28 months, so the personal bond date (first-time offender) is 15 July 2026. One-half is 42 months, so the bail date is 15 September 2027.
   Source: BNSS, 2023, Section 479 [[How to work out the Section 479 dates]]
5. The case began before 1 July 2024. Section 479 still applies: the Supreme Court held so in August 2024.
   Source: In Re: Inhuman Conditions in 1382 Prisons (Supreme Court, 2024) [[Section 479 applies to older cases too]]
6. Next: the jail superintendent must apply to the court. Send the Section 479 note to the jail legal aid clinic or the DLSA. The court decides; release is not automatic.
   Source: BNSS, 2023, Section 479(3) [[Who applies under Section 479]]

## Practice case 1: one case, one section (basics)
Core facts:
- A man in custody since 1 March 2025.
- Offence in January 2025, charged only under BNS 305 (theft in a dwelling house).
- Never convicted before; no other case pending; no delay caused by him.
Model answer:
- The offence was after 1 July 2024, so the BNS applies: BNS 305, maximum 84 months; no death or life imprisonment, so Section 479 can apply. [[How to work out the Section 479 dates]]
- One-third (28 months): personal bond date 1 July 2027. One-half (42 months): bail date 1 September 2028. [[How to work out the Section 479 dates]]
- He qualifies for free legal aid: he is in custody. [[legal_aid_eligibility.md#Who qualifies for free legal aid]]
- Next: the jail superintendent must apply; send the Section 479 note to the jail legal aid clinic or the DLSA. [[Who applies under Section 479]]
- Must not: promise release, or name a private lawyer.

## Practice case 2: a second case pending (advanced)
Core facts:
- A woman arrested on 1 December 2025 under BNS 318(4) (cheating and dishonestly inducing delivery of property).
- A second cheating case is pending against her in another district.
- No previous convictions; she has no lawyer; the family cannot afford sureties.
Model answer:
- BNS 318(4) has a maximum of 84 months, but a second case is pending, so Section 479 release is not available. She can still apply for ordinary bail. [[When Section 479 does not apply]]
- For a non-bailable offence the court decides bail; even where bail is usually refused, a woman may be released. [[Non-bailable offences: bail is the court's decision]]
- She qualifies for free legal aid: she is a woman, and she is in custody. Prepare the DLSA application. [[legal_aid_eligibility.md#Who qualifies for free legal aid]]
- If bail is granted but sureties cannot be arranged, the lawyer can ask the court to reduce the amount or accept a personal bond (BNSS Section 478). [[Bail granted but still in jail]]
- Flag for the lawyer: was the chargesheet filed within 60 days? If not, default bail may arise; it must be applied for before the chargesheet is filed. [[Default bail if police don't finish investigating in time]]
- Must not: promise release.

## Check (5 questions)
1. When does an accused person's right to a free lawyer begin?
   a) After the chargesheet is filed  b) When first produced before a magistrate  c) At the start of the trial  d) Only after conviction
   Answer: b
   Why: The right begins at the first production before a magistrate, who must tell the accused about it; they do not need to ask.
   Source: Khatri (II) v. State of Bihar (1981) [[Free legal aid starts at the first court appearance]]

2. For a bailable offence, bail is:
   a) a right  b) at the court's discretion  c) never given before trial  d) given only by the High Court
   Answer: a
   Why: For a bailable offence, bail is a right and the police or court must grant it.
   Source: BNSS, 2023, Section 478 [[Bailable offences: bail is a right]]

3. Under Section 479, a person never convicted before must be released on a personal bond after custody of:
   a) one-quarter of the maximum term  b) one-third  c) one-half  d) the full maximum term
   Answer: b
   Why: One-third for a first-time offender, on a personal bond; one-half for others, on bail.
   Source: BNSS, 2023, Section 479 [[Section 479: release after a share of the maximum sentence]]

4. Which of these is NOT covered by Section 479?
   a) Theft in a dwelling house (BNS 305)  b) Cheating (BNS 318(4))  c) Murder (BNS 103(1))  d) Theft, first conviction (BNS 303(2))
   Answer: c
   Why: Murder can be punished with death or life imprisonment, and Section 479 does not apply to such offences.
   Source: BNSS, 2023, Section 479 [[When Section 479 does not apply]]

5. Who has a legal duty to apply to the court under Section 479?
   a) The family  b) The police  c) The jail superintendent  d) The victim
   Answer: c
   Why: The jail superintendent must apply in writing once the one-third or one-half period is complete; the prisoner or their lawyer can also apply.
   Source: BNSS, 2023, Section 479(3) [[Who applies under Section 479]]
