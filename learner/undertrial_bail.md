# Learner module: undertrials and bail

Lawyer checked: no. Facts here must match knowledge/undertrial_bail.md and knowledge/sentence_table.csv.

## Explainer (read aloud, about 2 minutes)
Nearly three out of four people in Indian jails (73% at the end of 2024, according to the National Crime Records Bureau) are undertrials: their trial isn't over, so they have not been found guilty. Many are poor, and many stay inside longer than they should because nobody helps them use the rights they already have.

From the moment a person is first brought before a magistrate, they are entitled to a free lawyer if they can't afford one, and everyone in custody qualifies. For a bailable offence, bail is a right; if the person can't arrange bail within a week, the court can treat them as too poor and release them on a personal bond. For a non-bailable offence, the court decides.

Section 479 of the new criminal procedure law (BNSS) adds a time limit. An undertrial must be released on bail after spending half the maximum sentence for their offence in custody, or on a personal bond after one-third of it if they have never been convicted before. It doesn't apply to offences punishable with death or life imprisonment, or when the person faces more than one offence or more than one case. The jail superintendent must apply to the court, and the Supreme Court has said this covers older cases too.

Your job as a helper: collect the facts carefully, use the calculator, and get the case to the legal aid lawyer or jail legal aid clinic.

## Quiz (10 questions)

1. When does an accused person's right to a free lawyer begin?
   a) After the chargesheet is filed  b) When first produced before a magistrate  c) At the start of trial  d) Only after conviction
   Answer: b. Khatri (II) v. State of Bihar (1981): the duty begins at first production, and the magistrate must tell the accused.

2. A person in custody earns ₹6 lakh a year. Do they qualify for free legal aid?
   a) No, too much income  b) Yes, being in custody is enough  c) Only in the Supreme Court  d) Only if they are a woman
   Answer: b. Section 12(g) of the Legal Services Authorities Act covers people in custody whatever their income.

3. For a bailable offence, bail is:
   a) a right  b) at the court's discretion  c) never given before trial  d) only given by the High Court
   Answer: a. BNSS Section 478.

4. A poor accused can't arrange bail for a bailable offence within one week of arrest. What can the court do?
   a) Nothing  b) Presume they are indigent and release them on a personal bond  c) Transfer the case  d) Convict them
   Answer: b. BNSS Section 478.

5. Under Section 479, a first-time offender must be released on bond after custody of:
   a) one-quarter  b) one-third  c) one-half  d) the full maximum term
   Answer: b. One-third for a first-time offender; one-half for others.

6. Which of these cases is NOT covered by Section 479?
   a) Theft in a dwelling house (BNS 305)  b) Cheating (BNS 318(4))  c) Murder (BNS 103)  d) Extortion (BNS 308(2))
   Answer: c. Murder can be punished with death or life imprisonment.

7. Ravi is charged in two different cases. Can he use Section 479?
   a) Yes, in both  b) No; he must apply for ordinary bail  c) Yes, in the older case only  d) Only if one case is closed by the police
   Answer: b. Section 479(2) bars release when more than one case, or even more than one offence, is pending.

8. Who has a legal duty to apply to the court under Section 479?
   a) The family  b) The police  c) The jail superintendent  d) The victim
   Answer: c. Section 479(3). The prisoner or their lawyer can also apply.

9. The offence happened in March 2024. Can Section 479 still help?
   a) No, BNSS came into force later  b) Yes, the Supreme Court applied it to older cases too  c) Only if the trial started after July 2024  d) Only in Bihar
   Answer: b. Supreme Court, August 2024 (In Re: Inhuman Conditions in 1382 Prisons).

10. Police haven't filed a chargesheet 60 days after arrest for an offence punishable with up to 7 years. What right may arise?
    a) Default bail  b) Anticipatory bail  c) Automatic acquittal  d) Compensation
    Answer: a. Default bail under BNSS Section 187(3); the accused must apply before the chargesheet is filed.

## Practice case 1 (basics)
Case file for the agent to play:
- Speaker: the undertrial's elder sister, worried and unsure of dates at first.
- Facts (reveal only when asked): brother arrested 15 March 2024; offence in February 2024; charged under IPC 380 (theft in a dwelling house); never convicted before; no other cases; has a legal aid lawyer he has met twice; no delay caused by him; chargesheet filed in May 2024; bail never applied for.
Model answer:
- IPC 380 maps to BNS 305; maximum 7 years (84 months); no death or life; Section 479 can apply.
- First-time offender: one-third = 28 months → reached on 15 July 2026. Half = 42 months → 15 September 2027.
- Section 479 applies even though the offence was before July 2024 (Supreme Court, August 2024).
- He already qualifies for free legal aid (in custody).
- Next steps: Section 479 note to the jail legal aid clinic or DLSA (templates/section479_note.md); ask the legal aid lawyer to move the application; set a follow-up in 7 days.
- Must not: promise release, or name a private lawyer.

## Practice case 2 (advanced)
Case file for the agent to play:
- Speaker: an NGO caseworker.
- Facts: a woman arrested 1 December 2025 under BNS 318(4) (cheating and dishonestly inducing delivery of property); a second cheating case is pending in another district; no previous convictions; family can't afford bail sureties; she has no lawyer.
Model answer:
- BNS 318(4): maximum 7 years; but a second case is pending, so Section 479 release is not available.
- Route: regular bail under BNSS 480; a woman may be released even where the usual bars apply.
- She qualifies for free legal aid (woman, and in custody): DLSA application (templates/dlsa_application.md).
- If bail is granted but sureties can't be arranged: ask the lawyer to seek a personal bond or reduced surety (BNSS 478; Satender Kumar Antil).
- Flag for the lawyer: check whether the chargesheet was filed within 60 days (default bail, BNSS 187(3)).
