# Landmark judgments for learner mode

Checked: 2026-11-02 · Lawyer checked: no (every entry). Before launch, a lawyer or law teacher should check each summary and add the official citation and an Indian Kanoon or Supreme Court link.

## Free legal aid

### M.H. Hoskot v. State of Maharashtra (1978)
Free legal aid for a poor prisoner, including for an appeal, is part of the right to a fair procedure under Article 21. The state must provide a lawyer where the prisoner cannot afford one.
Module: legal aid · Track: basics and advanced

### Hussainara Khatoon v. Home Secretary, State of Bihar (1979)
Arose from undertrials held in Bihar jails for longer than the maximum sentence for their offence. The Supreme Court held that a speedy trial is part of the right to life and liberty (Article 21), ordered the release of such undertrials, and said the state must provide free legal aid to those who cannot afford a lawyer.
Module: legal aid, undertrial · Track: basics and advanced

### Khatri (II) v. State of Bihar (1981)
The state's duty to provide free legal aid begins when the accused is first produced before a magistrate, not only at trial. The magistrate must tell the accused that they have this right.
Module: legal aid, undertrial · Track: basics and advanced

### Suk Das v. Union Territory of Arunachal Pradesh (1986)
A conviction was set aside because the accused, who could not afford a lawyer, was never told about free legal aid. A poor accused person does not have to ask for legal aid for the right to apply.
Module: legal aid · Track: advanced

## Arrest and bail

### D.K. Basu v. State of West Bengal (1997)
Set out arrest safeguards: arresting officers must carry identification, prepare an arrest memo, inform a relative or friend, allow a medical examination, and produce the person before a magistrate. Many of these are now in the BNSS (Sections 35–58).
Module: undertrial · Track: basics and advanced

### Lalita Kumari v. Government of Uttar Pradesh (2013)
Police must register an FIR when information discloses a cognizable offence. A preliminary inquiry is allowed only in limited kinds of cases.
Module: undertrial, cyber fraud · Track: advanced

### Arnesh Kumar v. State of Bihar (2014)
Police should not arrest automatically for offences punishable with up to 7 years; they must record why arrest is necessary, and magistrates must check this before ordering detention. Now reflected in BNSS Section 35(3) (notice of appearance).
Module: undertrial · Track: basics and advanced

### Satender Kumar Antil v. Central Bureau of Investigation (2022)
Comprehensive bail guidelines: courts should not insist on conditions an accused cannot meet, should decide bail applications quickly, and should take care that poor undertrials are not kept in jail only because they cannot furnish sureties.
Module: undertrial · Track: advanced

### In Re: Inhuman Conditions in 1382 Prisons (Supreme Court orders, 2024)
In August 2024 the Court held that Section 479 BNSS applies to undertrials whose cases began before 1 July 2024, and directed jail superintendents to identify eligible prisoners and apply to courts. In November 2024 it asked for special efforts to identify eligible women prisoners.

Module: undertrial · Track: basics and advanced

## Domestic violence and maintenance (for the later module)

### Rajnesh v. Neha (2020)
Set uniform rules for maintenance cases, including a standard affidavit of assets and liabilities that both sides must file, and that maintenance is payable from the date of application.
Module: domestic violence (later) · Track: advanced

### Satish Chander Ahuja v. Sneha Ahuja (2020)
A woman's right to live in the "shared household" under the Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act can extend to a house owned by her in-laws.
Module: domestic violence (later) · Track: advanced

## Online speech and IT Act basics

### Shreya Singhal v. Union of India (2015)
Struck down Section 66A of the IT Act (offensive online messages) as unconstitutional. Useful background on which IT Act sections still apply (such as 66C and 66D for fraud).
Module: cyber fraud · Track: advanced
